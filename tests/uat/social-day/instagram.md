A model can now read a supplier invoice and draft the bill for you. Two write-ups came out today, one from the company and one from an independent reporter. Here is what it means if you run a small firm.

What happened: the feature reads the supplier, the date, the line items and the total from a scanned page or an emailed attachment. It drafts the bill in your books and marks anything it isn't sure about. Nothing is filed until a person has looked at it.

Why it matters now: most invoice work in a small office isn't hard. It is slow, and it arrives all at once at the end of the month. The independent report makes a useful point. The model does well on suppliers whose invoices always look the same, and it struggles with handwritten notes. So the honest promise is less typing, with checking kept.

Picture a small trading firm with one office manager. Today she types each invoice into the books on a Friday afternoon. With this on, she opens a list of drafts instead and reads each one against the page. She still decides what gets paid, and the books stay hers to run.

What to do this week: pick one supplier whose invoices all look alike. Try the feature on that supplier alone and note what it gets wrong. Then decide whether to widen it.
